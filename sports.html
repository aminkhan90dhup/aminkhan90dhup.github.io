Pakistan Shaheens reach the Rising Stars final — beat Bangladesh A by 5 wickets

Defending champions Pakistan Shaheens booked their place in Sunday's ACC Men's Asia Cup Rising Stars final in Doha on Friday 27 November, chasing down Bangladesh A's 232/8 with five wickets and 28 balls to spare. Bangladesh A, who won the toss and batted, were lifted by Shahadat Hossain's unbeaten 103 off 118 balls, but Sufyan Moqim's 3-29 — backed by two wickets each from Hasan Nawaz and Ali Raza — kept them to 232/8. The Shaheens slipped to 21/2 early, before Hasan Nawaz (62 off 91) and Shahzaib Khan (50 off 85) rebuilt with a 110-run third-wicket stand, and Farhan Yousaf (46 not out off 39) and captain Abdul Samad (44 off 33) finished the job at 233/5 in 45.2 overs. Hasan Nawaz was Player of the Match. The Shaheens now meet Sri Lanka A — eight-wicket winners over India A in Thursday's first semi-final — in Sunday's final.

Overton's 5-12 destroys Australia — England win 3rd T20I by 7 wickets (DLS) and lead 2–1

England took a 2–1 lead in the five-match T20I series in Brisbane on Friday 27 November, bowling Australia out for just 108 — their lowest T20I total at home — and winning a rain-affected chase by seven wickets under DLS. Jamie Overton took a career-best 5-12, including a double-wicket maiden, and Liam Dawson 3-16 as Australia collapsed from 65/2 to 108 all out in 16.5 overs after England won the toss and bowled; Sam Harper top-scored with 26. Rain delays cut England's target to 93 in 16 overs, and despite Riley Meredith's 3-30, Jordan Cox's unbeaten 33 off 14 balls and Aneurin Donald's 32 off 13 took England to 93/3 in 8.5 overs, with 43 balls to spare. Overton was Player of the Match. The fourth T20I is at the SCG in Sydney on Sunday 29 November.

Sri Lanka A storm into the Rising Stars final — beat India A by 8 wickets after twin centuries

Sri Lanka A reached the ACC Men's Asia Cup Rising Stars final in Doha on Thursday 26 November, chasing down India A's 317/8 with eight wickets and 8.3 overs to spare. Sri Lanka Women lift the Emerging Nations Trophy — beat Ireland by 6 wickets in the final

Unbeaten Sri Lanka Women clinched the ICC Women's Emerging Nations Trophy in Ajman on Wednesday 25 November, bowling Ireland out for 96 in 19.3 overs and chasing the target down in just 12.4 overs to win by six wickets. Sathya Sandeepani took 4/13 and was Player of the Final, while Imesha Dulani's unbeaten 44 off 25 balls sealed the chase at 99/4. Ireland, who lost captain Gaby Lewis in the opening over and slipped to 54/6, were held to 96 despite Alana Dalzell's 24. 15-year-old Sooryavanshi smashes record 204 off 53 balls — fastest List A century and double century

India A's Vaibhav Sooryavanshi, 15, rewrote the List A record books in Doha on Tuesday 24 November, hammering 204 off just 53 balls against UAE in the Asia Cup Rising Stars — 28 sixes and seven fours at a strike rate of 384.90, the most sixes ever in a senior men's innings. He reached his century in 28 balls, beating Jake Fraser-McGurk's 29-ball record, his 150 in 40 balls, and his double century in 52 balls, smashing Chad Bowes' previous 103-ball mark. Chasing 250, India A reached 254/3 in only 14.2 overs to win by seven wickets; UAE had posted 249/8, led by Harpreet Singh's 130. India A finished second in Group B behind Pakistan Shaheens and meet Sri Lanka A in Thursday's first semi-final.

New Zealand hold India to a draw in Wellington — 1st Test drawn, series 0–0

New Zealand batted out 120 overs on the final day to save the first Test against India at Basin Reserve, Wellington, on Monday 23 November. Pakistan beat Sri Lanka 1–0 to win the Test series — first series win of the WTC cycle

Pakistan wrapped up an eight-wicket win over Sri Lanka in the second Test in Rawalpindi on Friday 20 November to take the two-match series 1–0 — their first Test series win since beating England 2–1 at home in October 2024, and their first in the ICC World Test Championship 2025–27 cycle. The first Test at the same ground was drawn (Pakistan 434/9 declared and 284/8; Sri Lanka 446). In the second Test, Pakistan's pace attack bowled Sri Lanka out for 98 in 35.3 overs — the lowest Test total at Rawalpindi — before posting 325 for a 227-run lead. Sri Lanka fought back with 316 in their second innings (Milan Rathnayake 66 not out), leaving Pakistan 90 to win, which they reached in 13 overs for the loss of two wickets (Aamer Jamal 34 not out, Azan Awais 32 not out). Mohammad Abbas, who took 3-14 and 3-55, was Player of the Match.
