Bill Gates Warns AI-Powered Cyberattacks Are the Most Imminent AI Threat

Microsoft co-founder Bill Gates has warned that the most urgent danger from artificial intelligence today is not machines going out of control, but people misusing powerful AI tools. Speaking in an interview reported by Dawn, he said AI-powered cyberattacks are likely to increase sharply in the coming year, especially when safeguards are removed from the latest models.

Gates said self-regulation by AI companies alone is not enough and stronger limits are needed to stop misuse, including for cybercrime and bioterrorism. For students and teachers, the message is practical: strong passwords, careful sharing of personal data and healthy doubt about suspicious messages matter more than ever in the AI age.

More headlines: Amazon is reportedly in talks to buy AI startup Decart in a deal worth about $7 billion, PYMNTS reports citing The Wall Street Journal. Nvidia is exploring a bigger stake in, or a full acquisition of, open-source AI startup Reflection AI, according to the Financial Times via Economic Times. India's market regulator SEBI will soon issue guidelines for responsible AI use in capital markets, with human oversight and clear accountability, ANI reports via Economic Times.
